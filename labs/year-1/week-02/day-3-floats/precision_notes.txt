------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 2 - DAY 3: WHY FLOATS ARE WEIRD (LAB NOTES)
------------------------------------------------------------

1. THE 0.1 + 0.2 PROBLEM
Basically, computers are liars. We think 0.1 is a clean number, 
but in binary, it’s a repeating fraction—like 1/3 (0.333...) is for us. 
Since the computer only has 32 bits, it just "chops off" the end. 
When you add 0.1 and 0.2, those tiny "chopped off" errors add up. 
That’s why you get 0.3000000119... instead of a clean 0.3.

2. THE "EPSILON" RULE
Because of the rounding above, comparing floats with '==' is a trap. 
It’ll almost always return False.
- The Fix: Use FLT_EPSILON. 
- The Logic: Don't ask "Is it exactly 0.3?". Ask "Is the difference 
  between my result and 0.3 so tiny that it doesn't matter?" 
  If the gap is smaller than Epsilon, we call it "equal enough."

3. CATASTROPHIC CANCELLATION
This is "garbage in, garbage out." If you subtract two huge numbers 
that are almost identical (like 1.0000001 and 1.0000000), the 
accurate parts cancel out. You’re left with just the tiny rounding 
errors from the end, which is basically random noise. 
This kills accuracy in GPU rendering or physics stuff.
